# Sector Lenses

Research differs fundamentally by sector. Identify the sector first, then pull the matching lens. Applying the wrong lens (e.g. EV/EBITDA to a bank) produces confident nonsense.

## Banks / NBFCs / lenders

You are buying *how safely they lend*, not just how much they earn. Core metrics:

- **Net Interest Margin (NIM)** = net interest income ÷ average earning assets — the "gross margin" of a lender. Indian banks ~3%; NBFCs higher (~6–7%) but more volatile; unsecured lenders highest of all (yields can run 30%+ against low-teens cost of funds — high reward, high fragility).
- **Asset quality:** **Gross NPA** and **Net NPA** (loans 90+ days overdue), the **slippage ratio** (fresh NPA formation), and the **Provision Coverage Ratio (PCR)** (RBI expects total PCR not below ~70%; higher is more conservative).
- **CASA ratio** (current + savings ÷ total deposits) — high CASA = cheap funding = structural advantage (banks only). NBFCs can't take deposits, so scrutinise their **funding mix**: bank loans vs NCDs vs commercial paper vs securitisation, and the asset-liability maturity (ALM) match.
- **Capital adequacy (CAR / CRAR)** — the loss-absorption buffer; want headroom above the regulatory minimum (~11.5% Basel III for banks) to keep growing.
- **Credit cost** (provisions ÷ average loans), **cost-to-income ratio** (<50% is good), **ROA** (>1.3% good, >1.6% excellent for banks), **ROE** (>15%).

**Lender-specific traps:**
- A fast-growing, **unseasoned** loan book optically understates NPAs — borrowers rarely default in year one. Strong reported asset quality on a book that doubled recently means little yet.
- **Restructured assets** can be NPAs in disguise.
- For unsecured lenders, asset quality can deteriorate fast, and **regulation is a live risk** — the RBI's tightening of unsecured-lending risk weights and Digital Lending Directions directly slowed several players. Always read the latest CRISIL/ICRA rationale.

Valuation: P/B adjusted for ROE, plus P/E. A sub-1x book can be cheap *or* a warning that the market doubts the book's quality — distinguish which.

## FMCG / consumer

Driver: consumption demand. Watch:
- **Volume growth**, not just value/price-led growth — price-led growth without volume is unsustainable.
- Gross/operating margins and input-cost pressure.
- **Brand strength and pricing power**; distribution reach; rural-vs-urban mix.
- Customer/segment concentration for B2B-heavy players.

Context: Indian FMCG value growth has run ahead of volume; quick-commerce and regional/D2C players pressure incumbents' pricing power.

Valuation: high P/E and P/B are structural (brand value is off-balance-sheet); judge vs the company's own history.

## IT / tech services

Driver: global technology spending. Watch:
- **Deal pipeline / order book** and growth visibility.
- **Client concentration** (dependence on a few large clients = concentration risk).
- **Attrition** and utilisation (people are the main cost).
- Margin discipline; exposure to discretionary vs essential spend.

Main risk: client-spending slowdowns tied to global cycles.

## Manufacturing / industrials

Watch:
- **Capacity utilisation** and capex discipline (capex vs depreciation).
- **Working-capital intensity** (debtor/inventory days).
- Raw-material/commodity exposure; operating leverage; cyclicality.

Valuation: EV/EBITDA over a full cycle is more honest than a single-year P/E.
