# Data Sources (India-focused)

The edge for a retail investor in India is that institutional-grade primary documents are free. Paid screeners save time but are a convenience layer, not a substitute for reading the originals others skip.

## Free — and where the real edge is

**Screener.in** — the workhorse for fundamental analysis: 10-year financials, ratios, custom screens, downloadable Excel. Best for multi-year trend analysis and screening. Free core; Premium (~₹4,999/yr) adds custom queries and export. End-of-day data only.

**NSE (nseindia.com) and BSE (bseindia.com)** — official corporate filings: quarterly results, shareholding patterns, board-meeting outcomes, and **pledged-shares data**. Under SEBI LODR, companies file quarterly results within 45 days (audited annuals within 60). This is the *primary* source; everything else is downstream.

**SEBI (sebi.gov.in)** — the "Public Issues" section hosts every DRHP and RHP. SCORES is the investor-grievance system. The RHP/DRHP is the single most important document before any IPO.

**Company investor-relations pages** — annual reports, investor presentations, and **conference-call transcripts**. The Q&A in a concall is often more revealing than the scripted deck.

**Annual reports** — read the MD&A, notes to accounts, related-party transactions, contingent liabilities, and the auditor's report (look for "qualified opinion" or "emphasis of matter").

**Aggregators** — Trendlyne, Tickertape (free tier), Tijori, Trade Brains, Finology Ticker, Moneycontrol. Useful for quick summaries, transcripts, and shareholding/insider data.

## Credit-rating reports (free and underused)

CRISIL, ICRA, CARE, and India Ratings publish detailed **rating rationales** on their sites. For any company with debt — *especially* NBFCs/lenders — these assess asset quality, funding mix, liquidity (ALM), and management with a credit lens. Search the agency site for the company name.

## Paid (convenience, not necessity)

- **Screener Premium** (~₹4,999/yr) — deepest 10-year data, custom queries.
- **Tickertape Pro** (~₹2,399/yr) — clean UI, Investment Scorecard, unlimited screens; users report occasional data-accuracy gaps, so cross-check.
- **Trendlyne** — proprietary DVM (Durability/Valuation/Momentum) scores, forecasts, transcripts, alerts.
- **Tijori Finance** (~₹3,500/yr) — alternate/operational data (capacity, segment revenue, market share).

## Recommended free workflow

Screen on Screener/Trendlyne → narrow to a handful → read the latest annual report and 2–3 concall transcripts → pull filings/shareholding/pledge from NSE/BSE → read the relevant rating rationale → only then value.

## The RHP/DRHP (for IPOs)

The Red Herring Prospectus is the near-final pre-IPO document (the DRHP is the earlier draft filed for SEBI review; figures can change between them, so use the latest RHP). It omits only the final price (hence "red herring" — book-built IPOs show a price *band*). Key sections to read:

- **Risk Factors** — the legally-required honest list of what can go wrong. Most useful, most skipped.
- **Financials** — restated audited numbers (where widening losses or one-off revenue spikes show up).
- **Objects of the Offer** — what fresh money will actually be used for. Distinguish **fresh issue** (capital into the company) from **Offer for Sale / OFS** (existing holders cashing out — the company gets nothing).
- **Capital Structure / Selling Shareholders** — who's selling, how much, and their acquisition cost per share (a near-zero promoter cost vs your price band tells you something).
- **Promoter & shareholder details, related-party transactions.**

Caution: the **industry report** inside a DRHP is commissioned by the company and tends to be optimistic. Cross-check market-size and share claims.

## Caveats on data quality

Even reputable screeners contain occasional errors — cross-check critical numbers against primary filings. When two sources give materially different figures for the same metric (e.g. divergent P/E because of different trailing-earnings bases after a recent results announcement), say so and treat the valuation as uncertain rather than picking one silently. **GMP (grey market premium)** is unofficial, unregulated sentiment — never treat it as valuation.
